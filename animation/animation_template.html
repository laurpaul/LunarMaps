Closest opposing pairs

Separation is great-circle distance between marker centres. Zones touch when the radius reaches half the separation. Approximate positions are left out.

Sites

Elevation and slope from the 1/8° grid. Sunlight and Earth in view are the 0.25° cell averages for 2026. Nearest PSR is the distance to the closest cell the Sun never reached.

Terrain and layers

Terrain is a continuous surface from a LOLA-derived 1/8° elevation grid: 0.25° worldwide and a bicubic-smoothed polar grid (about 0.7 km spacing, interpolated) south of 75°S, coloured in 1.25 km bands with 250 m contour lines. Sunlight, Earth in view, slope and shadowed regions come from lunar_layers.py (JPL DE440, 2026, 3-hour steps, 0.25° cells), lightly smoothed and drawn as isobands on the GPU so their edges stay smooth at any zoom.

Sites and landers

The nine Artemis III regions NASA named in October 2024 (NASA), at LROC NAC mosaic centres where available; Mons Mouton at its summit. Chang'e-7: 88.8°S 123.4°E. ILRS: Amundsen and Malapert (Shi et al. 2022), Malapert placed at the massif's high point; Shackleton ridge, Nobile Rim 1 and Slater Plain are approximate. Landers are notional icons, not to scale.

Zones and plume

Zones are circles of the chosen radius, draped on the terrain; the slider covers 1 to 5 km, the range in the Paulson and Roberts safety-zone geometry paper (AIAA SciTech 2026). The plume launches 2,600 particles on ballistic arcs under lunar gravity (1.62 m/s²): landing range = v² sin 2θ / g. Arc heights are drawn 10× and time runs 6× fast. Shares quoted are of this illustrative spray only.
